If you want to sell lighting under your **own brand** — a showroom, a retail chain, an e-commerce store, a hotel spec house — you do not need to build a factory. You need a **private label lighting manufacturer**: a certified factory that builds well-engineered fixtures for you, then puts **your name** on the housing, the packaging, and the invoice. You own the brand and the market; the manufacturer owns the tooling, the production line, and the compliance.

## What Is a Private Label Lighting Manufacturer?

A **private label lighting manufacturer** produces lighting products that another company sells under **its own brand**. The manufacturer does the engineering, manufacturing, quality control, and certification. The buyer brings the brand name, the packaging design, and the go-to-market.

The phrase is used loosely, so it helps to pin down the four sourcing models that get mixed up:

| Model             | Who designs        | Who owns tooling | Your brand on it  | Best for                                      |
| ----------------- | ------------------ | ---------------- | ----------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| **Private label** | Factory (standard) | Factory          | Yes               | Resellers launching a brand at minimum cost   |
| **White label**   | Factory (standard) | Factory          | Neutral/unbranded | Same product sold under many brands           |
| **ODM**           | Factory (modified) | Factory          | Yes               | Brands that want a proven design adapted fast |
| **OEM**           | You (your spec)    | You / shared     | Yes               | Brands with their own IP, drawings, and specs |

The line between them blurs in practice. Most "private label lighting" requests are really **private label with a few meaningful customizations** — your logo on a certified product, plus your choice of finish, CCT, CRI, beam angle, wattage, dimming, and packaging. That is exactly what this guide covers, and it is the cheapest way to own a differentiated product without paying for full tooling.

## Why Go Private Label Instead of Building In-House?

The private label route exists because launching a lighting product line in-house is expensive and slow. A brand-new track light or downlight means molds, dies, thermal engineering, optics, driver qualification, and market-specific certification — easily 6–12 months and a six-figure budget before your first unit sells.

Private label compresses that to **weeks, not months**, and removes most of the fixed cost:

- **No tooling cost** — you start from the factory's proven, already-certified designs.
- **Faster time-to-market** — 4–8 weeks from sign-off to first shipment for standard product.
- **Proven reliability** — the design has already been field-tested; the factory has the test reports.
- **Low MOQ to test the market** — a small first run validates demand before you commit to volume.
- **Certifications that travel with the product** — ETL, CE, SAA test reports can be named to your SKU.

The trade-off is that you are not inventing a product — you are choosing from strong existing platforms and making them yours. For most brands, that is the right risk/reward. If you need a truly bespoke product engineered to your own drawings, that is the **OEM** route — see our [OEM LED track lighting guide](/oem-led-track-lighting-private-label-guide/) for the full 6-step process.

## What Can You Customize on Private Label Lighting?

This is where private label lighting earns its margin. A real manufacturer customizes the **light and the brand**, not just the label. Here is the full menu, from the parts that cost almost nothing to the options that change the product.

### Branding & packaging (品牌与包装)

The lowest-cost, highest-impact customization:

- **Logo placement** — laser engraving, silk screen, pad printing, or brand-color finishing on the housing.
- **Retail-ready packaging** — your box, your EAN/UPC barcode, spec stickers, foam inserts.
- **User manual & labels** — multilingual manuals, wiring diagrams, certification marks for your market.
- **Carton & outer packaging** — custom corrugated cartons, retail display-ready options.

### Housing color & material (外壳颜色与材质)

- **Colors** — any RAL/pantone: black, white, silver, matte, anodized, or custom brand colors.
- **Finishes** — powder coat, anodizing, electroplating, textured, sandblasted.
- **Materials** — die-cast aluminum (standard), extruded aluminum, or custom profiles for architectural or magnetic track systems.

### Light performance (光性能)

- **CCT (色温)** — 2700K, 3000K, 3500K, 4000K, or custom bins; SDCM ≤ 3 for color consistency across a retail chain. Use our [color temperature guide](/tools/cct-guide/) to pick the right CCT for your application.
- **CRI (显色指数)** — CRI 80/90/95+, with optional UV/IR filtering for artwork and merchandise. See our [CRI guide](/tools/cri-guide/) to set a color-rendering target before you write the spec.
- **Beam angle** — narrow spot (15°–24°), medium flood (30°–40°), wide flood / wall-wash (45°–60° and asymmetric). Calculate your own beam spread with our [beam angle calculator](/beam-angle-calculator/).

### Electrical & control (电气与控制)

- **Wattage** — typically 5W–50W per track head, or a custom driver for downlights.
- **Voltage** — 120V, 220–240V, 277V, or low-voltage (24V) magnetic track systems.
- **Dimming protocols** — TRIAC, 0–10V, DALI / DALI-2, or wireless (Zigbee, Casambi, Matter) for smart-home and e-commerce brands.

## The 6-Step Private Label Sourcing Process

A reliable private label project follows a defined sequence. Skip steps and you inherit the risk; follow them and you get a product that is buildable, certifiable, and on time.

### 1. Define your spec (确定规格)

Even on a standard product, you need a clear spec sheet before the factory quotes accurately. At minimum, send the factory:

- **Application** — retail, museum, hospitality, residential, office.
- **Photometrics** — beam angle, lumens, CCT, CRI, target lux.
- **Electrical** — voltage, wattage, dimming protocol.
- **Mechanical** — track type (3-wire, 1-circuit, magnetic), housing style, finish, dimensions.
- **Compliance market** — North America (ETL/UL), EU (CE), Australia (SAA), etc.

Not sure what numbers to specify? Our [photometric guide](/tools/photometric-guide/) and [lux calculator](/tools/lux-calculator/) turn your space into a spec.

### 2. Vet the factory, not the showroom (考察工厂而非展厅)

This is the step that separates a real **private label lighting manufacturer** from a trading desk:

- **Ask for workshop video** — assembly line, SMT machines, aging-test racks, OQC bench.
- **Ask who manufactures the driver and housing** — and whether thermal design is done in-house.
- **Check the address** maps to a real industrial park, not a shared office.
- **Review the [quality-control process](/quality-control/)** — a mature factory publishes how it inspects incoming materials, in-process production, and finished goods.

A factory that can only quote catalog items and cannot engineer a custom CCT, CRI, beam, or dimming profile is not doing private label — it is reselling.

### 3. Approve an engineered sample (确认样品)

For private label you are not always paying for a full prototype, but you should still demand a **production-grade sample** with:

- A **photometric (IES/LDT) file** so you can verify beam distribution in your design software.
- Measured **beam angle, lumens, CRI, and SDCM** data.
- A physical unit you can evaluate for finish, thermal behavior, and driver quality.
- Your **branding and packaging** applied as it will be in production.

Sample and branding approval typically takes **1–2 weeks**. This is where the spec is frozen — after approval, changes get expensive.

### 4. Lock certifications to your SKU (锁定认证)

Ask for **certificates and test reports that name your exact SKU and model**. A certificate for a similar product does not cover yours. For private label this matters more than people think — if the certificate names the factory's brand instead of yours, your customs clearance and your retail listings can stall.

- **ETL/UL** for North America, **CE (LVD+EMC)** for Europe, **SAA** for Australia/New Zealand, **ISO9001** for the factory.
- Ask which certificate documents ship with your consignment so customs clearance is smooth.

### 5. Confirm QC before shipment (出货前确认质检)

A real factory runs **IQC → IPQC → OQC** — incoming materials, in-process, and outgoing quality control — including a **100% aging test** (burn-in) before packing. For a private label order, verify:

- The final inspection covers electrical safety, lumen and CCT verification against the approved sample, and a **carton drop-test** on packaging.
- A third-party pre-shipment inspection company can visit or audit the goods if your volume justifies it.

### 6. Agree on shipping terms (确定交期与运输)

Your order is packed, labeled, and shipped per your terms — FOB, CIF, EXW, or DDP. You receive the compliance documents alongside the shipment so customs clearance is smooth.

## MOQ & Lead Times: What's Realistic?

### Realistic MOQs

| Product family              | Private label MOQ        |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| Standard track light        | 100–300 units per design |
| Standard downlight          | 100–300 units per design |
| Magnetic track system       | 200–500 units            |
| Custom branding / packaging | Usually separate, lower  |

MOQ should be negotiated per design and **itemized separately from tooling**. On a standard private label product there is usually no tooling cost, so the factory can start low and scale with you.

### Realistic lead times

| Stage                      | Time          |
| -------------------------- | ------------- |
| Sample + branding approval | 1–2 weeks     |
| Certification to your SKU  | 1–3 weeks     |
| Mass production + QC       | 2–4 weeks     |
| **First order, total**     | **4–8 weeks** |
| **Repeat order**           | **2–4 weeks** |

A factory that quotes "samples in a few days" without a dated production plan is telling you it does not engineer. For repeat orders, lead times compress because tooling and certification are already done.

## Common Payment Terms

- **30% deposit / 70% balance before shipment** — the industry standard for private label.
- **Deposit before sample**, balance before shipment for small orders.
- **Letter of credit (L/C)** for large or long-standing accounts.
- Payment via T/T (bank transfer) is most common; PayPal and credit card are often available for sample orders.

## FAQ

**What is a private label lighting manufacturer?**
A private label lighting manufacturer produces certified lighting products — track lights, downlights, magnetic systems — that another company sells under its own brand. The factory does the engineering, production, and quality control; the buyer owns the brand, the packaging, and the market.

**What is the difference between private label, white label, and OEM lighting?**
Private label means the factory's proven design carries your brand. White label is the same standard product sold unbranded under many brands. OEM means the product is engineered to your own spec with your own tooling — more control, more cost and lead time. Private label is the lowest-risk way to own a branded line.

**What is the MOQ for private label lighting?**
For standard product, private label MOQ often starts at 100–300 units per design, with branding and packaging itemized separately. It is usually lower than full OEM because there is no tooling cost to absorb. MOQ is negotiated per design and should be in writing before you pay a deposit.

**How long does a private label lighting order take?**
Sample and branding approval takes 1–2 weeks, certification to your SKU 1–3 weeks, and mass production 2–4 weeks — a realistic first order is 4–8 weeks, compressing to 2–4 weeks for repeat orders.

**What can I customize on private label lighting?**
Your logo and packaging, housing color and material, CCT (2700K–6500K), CRI (80/90/95+), beam angle, wattage, dimming protocol (TRIAC, 0–10V, DALI, wireless), and compliance for your market — all on a proven, already-certified platform.

**How do I verify a private label lighting factory is not a trading desk?**
Ask for workshop video (assembly line, SMT, aging-test racks), ask who manufactures the driver and housing, confirm the address is a real industrial park, review the quality-control process, and demand certificates that name your exact SKU. Then audit in person for a material order.

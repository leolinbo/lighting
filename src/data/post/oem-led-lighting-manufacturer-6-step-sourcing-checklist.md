Buying from an **OEM LED lighting manufacturer** is a partnership, not a purchase order. You are asking a factory to build, certify, and brand a product that carries your name — so the sourcing process has to be rigorous. This 6-step checklist is the exact framework we recommend B2B buyers use, whether you are sourcing [LED track lighting](/products/track-lighting/), [LED downlights](/products/led-downlights/), or a full commercial range.

Work through all six steps in order. Skipping one — especially certification or QC — is where sourcing projects go wrong.

## Step 1: Define Your Specification Before You Contact Anyone

The factory cannot quote accurately if you cannot describe the product. Before you send your first RFQ, write down:

- **Application** — retail, museum, hospitality, office, outdoor-adjacent.
- **Photometrics** — beam angle, lumens, CCT, CRI, target lux at a given mounting height.
- **Electrical** — voltage (120V, 220–240V, 277V), wattage, dimming protocol (TRIAC, 0-10V, DALI, Zigbee).
- **Mechanical** — track type, housing style, finish, dimensions.
- **Compliance market** — which certificates you need (ETL, CE, SAA) and where the product will be installed.

Not sure what numbers to specify? Use our free [beam angle calculator](/beam-angle-calculator/) and [lux calculator](/tools/lux-calculator/) to work out photometrics, and our [CCT guide](/tools/cct-guide/) and [CRI guide](/tools/cri-guide/) to lock color targets before you ask for a quote.

## Step 2: Vet the Factory, Not the Showroom

Now that your spec is clear, verify the candidate is genuinely an OEM LED lighting manufacturer and not a trading desk:

- **Ask for workshop video** — assembly line, SMT machines, aging-test racks, OQC bench. A factory will show you; a trader cannot.
- **Ask who manufactures the driver and housing** — and whether thermal design is done in-house.
- **Check the factory address** maps to a real industrial park, not a shared office.
- **Look at the [quality-control process](/quality-control/)** — a mature factory publishes how it inspects incoming materials, in-process production, and finished goods.

A red flag is a supplier that can only quote catalog items and cannot engineer a custom CCT, CRI, beam, or dimming profile. A genuine OEM LED lighting manufacturer customizes the light, not just the label.

## Step 3: Demand an Engineered Sample With Photometric Data

A sample is not a sticker on a catalog product. For a real OEM project, the factory should build an **engineered prototype** that ships with:

- A **photometric (IES/LDT) file** so you can verify distribution in your design software.
- Measured **beam angle, lumens, CRI, and SDCM** data.
- A physical unit you can evaluate for finish, thermal behavior, and driver quality.

Prototyping typically takes **2–3 weeks**, with one or two revision rounds before approval. This is the point where the spec is frozen — after approval, changes become expensive because tooling and production parameters are locked.

## Step 4: Verify Certifications Name Your Product

This is the step most buyers rush, and the one that hurts the most if wrong. Ask for **certificates and test reports that name your exact SKU and model**. A certificate for a similar product does not cover yours.

- **ETL/UL** for North America, **CE (LVD+EMC)** for Europe, **SAA** for Australia/New Zealand, **ISO9001** for the factory.
- Ask which certificate documents will ship with your consignment so customs clearance is smooth.
- If the product needs market-specific testing (e.g., DLC for utility rebates in North America), confirm who covers the cost and timeline.

## Step 5: Audit the QC Process and Aging Test

Your brand ships in every carton, so QC is non-negotiable. Confirm the OEM LED lighting manufacturer runs a four-stage loop:

1. **IQC** — incoming quality control on LED bins, drivers, and housings per AQL.
2. **IPQC** — in-process quality control on the line.
3. **100% aging test** — every fixture burned in before packing to catch early failures.
4. **OQC** — final function and electrical testing before sealing.

For a material order, consider a third-party inspection or an in-person audit. Full traceability — tracking codes linking a shipped unit to its production batch — is a hallmark of a mature factory. Read how we run [factory and quality control](/quality-control/) at our Shenzhen plant.

## Step 6: Negotiate MOQ, Lead Time, and Payment in Writing

Before you pay a deposit, get three things itemized in writing:

- **MOQ** — commonly 500 units per model for OEM orders, with flexible trial options. Tooling is usually itemized separately.
- **Lead time** — samples in 7–15 days, bulk in 25–45 days. A full first OEM cycle with tooling and certification is realistically 8–14 weeks.
- **Payment terms** — the industry standard is 30% deposit / 70% before shipment (T/T), with L/C for large accounts. Samples are usually paid upfront.

A supplier that avoids written commitments on MOQ and tooling, or that quotes an unrealistically short lead time, is likely not building your product. Our [OEM/ODM manufacturing page](/oem-odm/) explains the full customization menu and process.

## FAQ

**What is an OEM LED lighting manufacturer?**
A factory that produces LED lighting to your specification under your brand — handling engineering, production, quality control, and compliance, while you own the brand and market.

**How is OEM different from ODM?**
OEM builds a product to your exact spec with your brand; ODM adapts one of the factory's existing catalog designs and brands it for you. OEM gives more control but usually needs tooling; ODM is faster and cheaper to start.

**What is the typical MOQ for OEM LED lighting?**
Around 500 units per model for OEM orders, with flexible trial options. Bespoke tooling may raise it; ODM modifications may lower it. Always get it itemized in writing.

**How long does an OEM lighting order take?**
Samples in 7–15 days, bulk in 25–45 days, and a full first cycle with tooling and certification 8–14 weeks. Repeat orders compress to 4–6 weeks.

**What certifications do I need?**
ETL/UL for North America, CE for Europe, SAA for Australia/New Zealand. Confirm certificates name your exact SKU.
